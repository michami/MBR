
## The Dice

Ember uses a single long **d4** die. AKA: Stick die. It is cut from a stick with straight edges and a square cross section. Leave one side blank, then cut **1**, **3**, and **2** notches, in that order, on the other sides. Each notch will wrap around **2** sides and opposite sides will add up to **3**.

Each number of the die has a meaning, is connected to one of the **4** elements, and represents one of your **4** stats. By itself, a single die can act as a simple oracle that you roll to describe a situation or person.

**0** is earth, your constitution (**CON**), The smooth surface is the face of a stone. Your foundation. This means your past or family. Someone with a down to earth personality. They will likely help out of responsibility.

**1** is water, your strength (**STR**), the single line is a river or a friend who stands with you. Someone with an outgoing personality. They will likely help because they like you.

**2** is wind, your dexterity (**DEX**), two lines showing the blowing air. It's also two lovers, someone who's passionate, loving, and caring, but maybe not to you. Possibly a pacifist. They will likely NOT help you because you're not the one they care most about.

**3** is fire, your mind and magic (**INT**), the lines showing the tips of a flame. It's also a group of people, an army or mob, representing a danger. They will not help you, and may even try to sabotage your efforts.

## Rolling

Roll **3** dice once, or a single die **3** times and add the results. Any time you roll all the same number, it represents a plot twist in the current scene. You then add to the results any relevant stats or backgrounds as explained later. If the result is equal to or greater than **10**, you succeed.

No bonus added to the roll can be less than **0** or greater than **9**. This means there's always the chance of a failure, even with a **+9** to the roll.  (If you roll a **0**)

Keep in mind: A **+5** bonus has a **50%** chance of success. Everything else is relative to this.

## A note about numbers

A roll of **0**, is always a failure. The range of **3** to **7** are fairly evenly distributed in the type of roll that this game uses. Numbers outside this range are unlikely to be rolled, making smaller values too difficult and larger ones to easy.

## Stats

Your character starts with **5** points for each of the **4** stats indicated by the dice: **CON**, **STR**, **DEX**, and **INT**. You may distribute the points as you wish between the stats, but each stat must have no less than **3** points and no more than **7** points. You start with **5** points per stat and if evenly distributed, there's a **50%** chance of a success on all rolls using only the stats.

You get **HP** tokens equal to **5** plus your **CON**. Any damage you take will be subtracted from your **HP**.

Your character will also start with one **background** which is applied contextually. The **background** usually adds **+1** to a relevant roll that uses that **background** but can add **+2** for a highly specific **background**. For example, a thief can pick locks, sneak in shadows, pick pockets, etc. so they only add **+1** when picking locks. But a locksmith's skills are only used when picking locks, so they would add **+2** when picking locks. If you have multiple relevant backgrounds, (as described later) you may add **+2**, but no more.

Use the stats in a way that makes sense. For example, if you're trying to talk someone into giving you a deal using:

* Your good looks, roll **+CON**
* Intimidation, roll **+STR**
* Slight of hand, roll **+DEX**
* Deception, roll **+INT**

## Luck Tokens

Every impactful failed roll gives you a luck token. You can replace any roll, except a progress roll, with your total luck tokens. If you do, reset your tokens back to **0**. You cannot accumulate more than **9** luck tokens.

## Progress

When you have some task or goal you need to make progress towards, you will create a progress tracker. The progress tracker has an "Ease to Progress" (**EP**) which indicates how easy it is to add to the progress.  Each time you make an action to further your progress, you will add the **EP** to your roll to see if you can add a point to the progress.

The **EP** can change depending on the action you're taking. For example: Finding a map showing the location of an item you're trying to find would have a higher **EP** than hearing rumors about where the item may be.

For every **2** points over **10** on your final result, (roll + **EP**) you can add another point of progress.

For example, if the **EP** is **6** and you roll a **6**, your final score is **12**, and you can add **2** points to your progress. (**1** point for a successful roll, plus another point for getting **2** over **10**)

You cannot make more than **9** progress.

Once you feel that your progress is high enough, you will make a progress roll.

## Progress Roll

A progress roll is just like a normal roll, only you add your current progress to the dice instead of a stat or background. If you fail the roll, then you subtract the difference from your progress.  For example, if you have **5** progress and you roll a **3**, for a total of **8**, then you fail the roll and subtract **2** (**10** - **8**) from your progress, bringing it down to **6**. If you fail a roll, you need to make at least **1** more point towards your progress before you can make another progress roll.

You cannot use luck on a progress roll.

## Combat

Combat is tracked as progress against an enemy. The **EP** of the progress represents how easy it is to defeat the enemy. Each time you land an attack on an enemy, you roll to add progress.  Large groups of enemies can use a single progress track.

After each attack, weather it's successful or not, you will then roll for the enemy to attack you, if they're able.

Melee attacks roll **+STR**

Ranged attacks roll **+INT**

Dodge attacks roll **+DEX**

If you fail to dodge an attack, you lose **1 HP**. If any attack would drop your **HP** to **0** or less, you need to make a saving roll **+CON**. If you fail, you die. If you succeed, you remain at **0** and must make another saving roll every time you take damage.

While in combat, any action can result in an attack from an enemy. Taking a healing potion or running away gives the enemy a change to strike. Every turn of combat allows for one action by the PC and one reaction by the enemy. If you're playing with multiple PCs or enemies, they each get an action if it makes sense for them to.

## Magic

When you cast magic, you imagine the effect you're trying to accomplish, within your skills, and roll **+INT**. If the magic can scale up, then for every **2** points you roll over **10**, you do an additional level of effect.

Magic is typically a background, though it can come from an item. It uses the same rules as any other background.  ie: A background of "Fire Mage" would give a **+1** to all fire spells, but a background of "Fire Ball Mage" would give a **+2** to the "Fire Ball" spell specifically.

## Rests

Between combats, you take a short rest and recover **HP**. Roll **3** dice to determine how many **HP** to recover.

## Leveling

You do not gain stats by leveling. If you wish, you may gain weapons at the end of an adventure that act like a background.

Additional backgrounds can be learned after going on an adventure to get the resources needed to learn a new skill.

## Inventory

Ember uses a "**quantum inventory**". It is assumed your PC is more knowledgeable on what they should bring on a trip than you are. Because of that, the player doesn't know what the PC has until it is needed. When an item is needed, you can either assume the PC has the item, or roll to see if the PC had the foresight to bring the item.

For example, you can assume your PC has rope, so you don't need to roll for it. They probably brought extra climbing equipment, so you can roll with a plus **6** or **7**. They probably didn't bring a chisel and hammer, but it's possible, so roll with a plus **3** or **4**.

Similarly, you can roll to see if an item they have is the right item, such as if a key they found opens a specific door or chest.

## Relationships

There are **4** relationship types, matching the **4** sides of the die:

* Family
* Friend
* Romantic
* Enemy

There are also **4** levels for each relationship type:

| Level | Description                   |
|-------|-------------------------------|
| 0     | Casual knowledge of           |
| 1     | Personal knowledge of         |
| 2     | Spends time together          |
| 3     | Spends intimate time together |

Examples for each:

| Type     | 0                  | 1                        | 2                | 3                              |
|----------|--------------------|--------------------------|------------------|--------------------------------|
| Family   | Cousin             | Grandparent              | Parent           | Sibling                        |
| Friendly | Know Their Name    | Know Personal Information| Hang Out Together| Self Sacrificing Friendship    |
| Romantic | Flirting           | Dating                   | Engaged          |                         Married|
| Enemy    | Part Of Enemy Team | Known Enemy Member       | Personal Enemy   | Actively Trying To Kill        |

Your relationship to others is recorded as:

* The relationship type
* The relationship level
* The progress to the next level

For example, if you're dating someone and have made significant progress towards asking them to marry you, you could record this as:

(**2**) (**1**) (**5**)

(**2**: romantic) (**1**: dating) (**5** progress towards being engaged)

These numbers would be recorded on that npc's stats. (They record their relationship with you)

## Recording Stats

Since all stats have a specific order and value, they can be recorded as notches on a stick, knots in a rope, or any other convenient way to record a list of numbers. I prefer knots and use a figure 8 knot to represent a **0** value, single overhand knots for a **1** value, and a double overhand knot for a **5** value in roman numeral notation. I personally like to record stats in this order:

**Description:** (Especially useful for NPCs)

* Size (abstract, relative number)
* Gender (0=Male, 3=Female)
* Race (See races)

**Relationship to PC:** (NPCs only, may have multiple sets)

* Type
* Level
* Progress to next level

**Stats:**

* **CON**
* **STR**
* **DEX**
* **INT**

An NPC that has all three of these sets of values could be recorded on 3 lengths of string, each tied in a row on a stick or another piece of string.

Backgrounds cannot be recorded easily, which is why each PC only starts with **1** and must put effort into gaining additional backgrounds. It makes it easier to remember. If you want, you could carve a symbol or object in wood to represent a character's background. For example, a sword for a fighter, a book for a magic user, or a key for a thief.

## Additional oracles

You can use these tables, or if you don't have them with you, make a 4x4 grid and draw images or single words into each box. Try to make the words or icons generic enough to have multiple generic meanings. Oracles are hints at what needs to be done and can have multiple meanings. For example, "Follow + Trap" could mean:

* Find someone by following the trail of traps they laid out to protect their hideout
* Follow some instructions on building or placing traps
* Follow a person and lay a trap for them for when they return
* Follow a map to a treasure, but watch out for traps
* Follow a person, but unknown to your character, you're being led into a trap

# Setting

While the rules are designed to be setting neutral, I did create a high fantasy setting with magic and races that I'll explain here if you want to use it.

## Races

Ember has 4 races, each represented by one of the elements. Each race has their own type of magic they're capable of. If using a racial magic without a background, roll on **+INT** without any other bonus.

#### Dwarf (Earth)

Dwarfs mine the Earth and work the metal into shape using the other elements. Fire to heat it, wind to stoke the fire, and water to quench the metal. Dwarfs are tough like stone and have a strong connection to their families and their past.

Elemental (earth, water, wind, fire) magic. Durable elemental enhanced weapons. Clay golums.

#### Human (Water)

Humans live on the Earth and use the forces of rivers to power their machinery and water their fields. They are the experts an manipulating energy to their will.

Electrical magic. Electronics. Constructs. Complex mechanical traps. Complex weapons such as firearms, crossbows, etc. All humans can power electrical devices with their magic, or anything that requires a spark.

#### Elf (Wind)

Elves live in the trees. They're light and fast, able to jump from branch to branch. Because of their long lives, elves do not believe in monogamy, so their relationships are fast and fleeting.

Nature / spiritual magic. Potions. Healing magic. Plant golems. Sentient / living weapons.

#### Beast-Kin (Fire)

Beast-Kin are half animal and can change fully into their animal shapes. They are more feral than the other races, which can make them dangerous when their instincts take over.

Self transformation / boosting magic. All beast-kin have some understanding of other animals' intents, can communicate with the same type of animal as their animal form, can heal themselves, and can transform into their one associate animal (a cat girl changes into a cat) but they need a background to change into other animals. (a cat girl that can change into a bear, or bird)

Their animal form will have its own stats at the same level as the human form. For example, a **5 3 3 9** (**HP 10**) bear-kin may change their stats to **10 6 3 1** (**HP 15**) when transformed into a bear.

Transformations always succeed.

Each form has their own instance of tokens and damage. They are played like two separate entities. If one form drops to **0 HP**, they transform into their other form automatically and cannot change back until a long rest, or a specialty spell / potion that is specifically made to heal their other form is used. Rests affect both forms equally, but healing only heals the current form.

**INT** typically drops to **1** in animal form (the only time a stat can drop below **3**) as the animal form has barely human intelligence and is susceptible to mental attacks.

Other races that can transform, like a half-pixie that changes between human and pixie form, would be played like a beast-kin.
