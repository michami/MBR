
## The Dice

Ember uses a single long **d4** die. AKA: Stick die. It is cut from a stick with straight edges and a square cross section. Leave one side blank, then cut **1**, **3**, and **2** notches, in that order, on the other sides. Each notch will wrap around **2** sides and opposite sides will add up to **3**.

Each number of the die has a meaning and connected to one of the **4** elements, and one of your **4** stats.

**0** is earth, your constitution (**CON**), a smooth stone. Your foundation. This means your past or family. Someone with a down to earth personality. Will likely help out of responsibility.

**1** is water, your strength (**STR**), the single line is a river or a friend who stands with you. Someone with an outgoing personality. Will likely help because they like you.

**2** is wind, your dexterity (**DEX**), two lines showing the blowing air. It's also two lovers, someone who's passionate, loving, and caring, but maybe not to you. Possibly a pacifist. Will likely NOT help because you're not the one they care about.

**3** is fire, your mind and magic (**INT**), the lines showing the tips of a flame. It's also a group of people, an army or mob, representing a danger. Will not help you.

## Rolling

Roll the dice once, or a single die **3** times. Add the results. If you roll all **3**s, roll a single die again and add the result. Keep rolling for every **3** rolled.

Any time you roll **3** of the same number, it represents a plot twist in the current scene.

Typically, you're rolling to match or beat a defined "Difficulty Class", or **DC**. In some cases, you'll roll under the **DC** if the **DC** is defined by your stats or progress.

## Stats

Your character starts at level **5**. You get **1** token per stat per level. Since you have **4** stats, you start with **20** tokens to divide between your stats. No stat can be lower than **half** your level, rounded up. So when you start at level **5**, no stat can be less than **3**.

You get **HP** tokens equal to your level plus your **CON**. Any damage you take will be subtracted from your **HP**. If any attack would drop your **HP** to **0** or less, you need to make a saving throw on your **CON**. If you fail, you die.

Your character will also start with one **background** which is applied contextually. The **background** usually adds **1** to a relevant roll or **DC** that uses that **background**, but can add **2** for a highly specific **background**. For example, a thief can pick locks, sneak in shadows, pick pockets, etc. so they only add **1** when picking locks. But a locksmith's skills are only used when picking locks, so they would add **2** when picking locks.

Use the stats in a way that makes sense. For example, if you're trying to talk someone into giving you a deal using:

* Your good looks, roll on **CON**
* Intimidation, roll on **STR**
* Slight of hand, roll on **DEX**
* Deception, roll on **INT**

## Luck

Every impactful failed roll gives you a luck token. You can replace any "match or beat" roll with your total luck tokens. If you do, remove all tokens.

## Defining The Difficulty Class

A **DC** of **5** has a **50%** chance of success. This is the default value when all things are equal. You increase the **DC** for a harder challenge, and decrease it for an easier challenge.

If you're rolling on competing stats, you would subtract one stat from the other, and shift the **DC** up or down by the difference.

For example: you want to attack with **STR** of **3** and your target is trying to avoid you with a **DEX** of **5**. That's a difference of **2**. Since this makes the attack more difficult, you would increase the **DC** by **2** to get **7**.

If you are rolling against your stat, but you don't have another stat to compete against, then you try to roll under your stat. This means a stat of **5** would have a **50%** chance of success with no other information available. This is why your character starts at level **5**, it means that if your stats are evenly spread, you have a **50%** chance of success at most tasks.

## Progress

When you have some task or goal you need to make progress towards, you will create a progress tracker. The progress tracker has a **DC** which indicates how hard it is to add to the progress.  Each time you make an action to further your progress, you will roll on the **DC** to see if you can add a point to the progress.

The **DC** can change depending on the action you're taking. For example: finding a map for an item would have a lower DC for making progress than hearing rumors about where the item may be.

For every **2** points over the **DC** you roll, you can add another point to the progress.

For example, if the progress **DC** is **4** and you roll a **6**, you can add **2** points to your progress.

Once you feel that your progress is high enough, you will make a progress roll.

## Progress Roll

In a progress roll, the roll is competing against your progress, so the progress is your score and the roll becomes the **DC** for that score. This means you're hoping to roll under your progress value.

On a failure, subtract the difference from your progress. Something went wrong and you need to regain some progress before you can roll again.

Example: You made **6** points of progress in finding the sword of MacGuffin. You roll the dice and hope to roll under a **6**. You roll an **8**. This means you failed the roll and must subtract **2** points (**8** - **6**) from your progress. This brings your progress down to **4**. In the fiction of your story, you could explain that the map saying the sword was in the tower was outdated and it must have been moved. Maybe another adventurer found it long ago and sold it?

## Combat

Combat is tracked as progress against an enemy. The **DC** of the progress is the difficulty of beating the enemy. Each time you land an attack on an enemy, you roll to add progress to defeating that enemy.  Large groups will often have a single progress track.

After each attack, weather it's successful or not, you will then roll for the enemy to attack you, if they're able. If the enemy lands a hit, you lose 1 HP.

## Rests

Between combats, you take a short rest and recover **HP** equal to the **DC** of the combat.

## Leveling

Leveling is rare and only occurs after a major event. Think: Gandalf vs the Balrog.

When you increase in level, you get 4 more points for your stats. (1 per stat per level)

The minimum stat value and your maximum luck will also change on each odd numbered level increase. (Level 7, 9, etc.)

Additional backgrounds can be learned after going on an adventure to get the resources needed to learn a new skill.

## Inventory

Ember uses a "**quantum inventory**". It is assumed your PC is more knowledgeable on what they should bring on a trip than you are. Because of that, the player doesn't know what the PC has until it is needed. When an item is needed, you can either assume the PC has the item, or roll on a **TR** to see if the PC had the foresight to bring the item.

For example, you can assume your PC has rope, so you don't need to roll for it. They probably brought extra climbing equipment, so you can roll on a low **TR**, maybe **3** or **4**. They probably didn't bring a chisel and hammer, but it's possible, so roll on a high **TR**, maybe **6** or **7**.

Similarly, you can roll to see if an item they have is the right item, such as if a key they found opens a specific door or chest.

## Relationships

There are 4 relationship types, matching the 4 sides of the die:

* Family
* Friend
* Romantic
* Enemy

There are also 4 level for each relationship type:

| Level | Description                   |
|-------|-------------------------------|
| 0     | Casual knowledge of           |
| 1     | Personal knowledge of         |
| 2     | Spends time together          |
| 3     | Spends intimate time together |

Examples for each:

| Type     | 0                  | 1                        | 2                | 3                              |
|----------|--------------------|--------------------------|------------------|--------------------------------|
| Family   | Cousin             | Grandparent              | Parent           | Sibling                        |
| Friendly | Know Their Name    | Know Personal Information| Hang Out Together| Self Sacrificing Friendship    |
| Romantic | Flirting           | Dating                   | Engaged          |                         Married|
| Enemy    | Part Of Enemy Team | Known Enemy Member       | Personal Enemy   | Actively Trying To Kill        |

Your relationship to others is recorded as:

* The relationship type
* The relationship level
* The progress to the next level

For example, if you're dating someone and have made significant progress towards asking them to marry you, you could record this as:

(2) (1) (5)

(romantic) (dating) (5 progress to being engaged)

These numbers would be recorded on that npc's stats. (They record their relationship with you)

## Recording Stats

Since all stats have a specific order and value, they can be recorded as notches on a stick, knots in a rope, or any other convenient way to record a list of numbers. I prefer knots and use a figure 8 knot to represent a **0** value, single overhand knots for a **1** value, and a double overhand knot for a **5** value in roman numeral notation. I personally like to record stats in this order:

**Description:** (Especially useful for NPCs)

* Size (abstract, relative number)
* Gender (0=Male, 3=Female)
* Race (See races)

**Relationship to PC:** (NPCs only, may have multiple sets)

* Type
* Level
* Progress to next level

**Stats:**

* **CON**
* **STR**
* **DEX**
* **INT**

An NPC that has all three of these sets of values could be recorded on 3 lengths of string, each tied in a row on a stick or another piece of string.

Backgrounds cannot be recorded easily, which is why each PC only starts with **1** and must put effort into gaining additional backgrounds. It makes it easier to remember. If you want, you could carve a symbol or object in wood to represent a character's background. For example, a sword for a fighter, a book for a magic user, or a key for a thief.

# Setting

While the rules are designed to be setting neutral, I did create a high fantasy setting with magic and races that I'll explain here if you want to use it.

## Races

Ember has 4 races, each represented by one of the elements. Each race has their own type of magic they're good at.

#### Dwarf (Earth)

Dwarfs mine the Earth and work the metal into shape using the other elements. Fire to heat it, wind to stoke the fire, and water to quench the metal. Dwarfs are tough like stone and have a strong connection to their families and their past.

Elemental (earth, water, wind, fire) magic. Durable elemental enhanced weapons. Clay golums. All dwarfs can use their magic to mildly boost elements. For example, they can make things tougher or resistant to being moved, extract water, make wind blow harder, and make fire hotter.

#### Human (Water)

Humans live on the Earth and use the forces of rivers to power their machinery and water their fields. They are the experts an manipulating energy to their will.

Electrical magic. Electronics. Constructs. Complex mechanical traps. Complex weapons such as firearms, crossbows, etc. All humans can power electrical devices with their magic, or anything that requires a spark.

#### Elf (Wind)

Elves live in the trees. They're light and fast, able to jump from branch to branch. Because of their long lives, elves do not believe in monogamy, so their relationships are fast and fleeting.

Nature / spiritual magic. Potions. Healing magic. Plant golems. Sentient / living weapons. All elves can passively listen to plants and animals and have healing magic.

#### Beast-Kin (Fire)

Beast-Kin are half animal and can change fully into their animal shapes. They are more feral than the other races, which can make them dangerous when their instincts take over.

Self transformation / boosting magic. All beast-kin have some understanding of other animals' intents, can communicate with the same type of animal as their animal form, can heal themselves, and can transform into their one associate animal (a cat girl changes into a cat) but they need a background to change into other animals. (a cat girl that can change into a bear, or bird)

Their animal form will have its own stats at the same level as the human form. For example, a **5 3 3 9** (**HP 10**) bear-kin may change their stats to **10 6 3 1** (**HP 15**) when transformed into a bear.

Transformations always succeed.

Each form has their own instance of tokens and damage. They are played like two separate entities. If one form drops to **0 HP**, they transform into their other form automatically and cannot change back until a long rest, or a specialty spell / potion that is specifically made to heal their other form is used. Rests affect both forms equally, but healing only heals the current form.

**INT** typically drops to **1** in animal form as the animal form has barely human intelligence and is susceptible to mental attacks. **INT** may be used in animal form for healing or to represent an instinctive reaction to shift a roll.

Beast-kin healing is a single action in either form, as they have natural fast healing.

Other races that can transform, like a half-pixie that changes between human and pixie form, would be played like a beast-kin.
